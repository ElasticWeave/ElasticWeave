# Heterogeneous team management

Read this when choosing members, briefs, parallel width, integration ownership, or reassignment.
These are revisable operating hypotheses drawn from organizational and team research, not evidence
that a model brand is globally stronger or that adding agents improves this project's outcomes.

## Judge capability on the assignment

Distinguish task competence, useful autonomy, ambiguity handling, instruction/tool reliability,
coordination, and honest reporting of uncertainty. A good local solver may be a poor integrator;
a reliable collector may lack the evidence needed to judge a critical conclusion. Model labels,
role names, price, and live-catalog eligibility do not demonstrate any of these capabilities.
"Qualified" means suitable task evidence within the current role, route, effort, and review policy.

Use relevant prior work or an early sample of the actual assignment. If uncertainty affects routing,
start with a small real, low-risk, observable assignment and check its evidence before expanding
scope or fan-out. Unknown is not weak. Do not build a complete benchmark or authenticate unused
routes at cold start. Reuse the existing brief and evidence record; add no mandatory capability
score, ledger, or startup gate. Fresh per-dispatch resolution still authorizes every selected route;
partial route coverage never makes the whole catalog snapshot `verified`.

## Keep authority separate from expertise

The user retains goals, acceptance criteria, permissions, and authorization for their changes.
The coordinator organizes work and interfaces within that authority; organizational position does
not imply superior expertise. The existing terminal owner retains lifecycle and pending-work
responsibility. Only the selected independent reviewer issues its scoped terminal verdict under
the existing review contract; a solver, reducer, panel, or coordinator cannot self-certify completion.
Machine-gated review retains its own admitted proof contract.

Give members method autonomy inside explicit scope, interfaces, constraints, and evidence duties.
Successful calibration can justify a broader authorized assignment; it never expands permissions,
changes acceptance, overrides the reviewer, or transfers top-session mutation rights. An expert
must be able to challenge a supervisor's premise with evidence. Carry that disagreement to the
appropriate decision owner instead of requiring compliance with a demonstrably failed assumption.

## Three assignment patterns

**Experts on this task.** Specify the outcome, acceptance signals, interfaces, relevant context,
constraints, and integration owner. Let each expert choose the method; prescribe steps only where
a concrete safety or interface invariant needs them. Review evidence and consequential decisions
without redoing every step. When independent judgment adds value, collect proposals before
cross-exposure; reviewers still receive the actual work and evidence they must inspect.

**Experts plus support.** Experts own ambiguous design, critical inference, conflict resolution,
and difficult semantic integration within their assigned authority. Give support members explicit
inputs, allowed files/tools, output interfaces, checks, and an exception route; add examples when
they resolve likely ambiguity. Allow implementation choices inside that contract. Retrieval,
mechanical edits, predetermined runs, and evidence formatting are useful support assignments when
their outputs are checkable. A new architectural question or unsupported critical conclusion
returns to a qualified owner. "Missing context", "failed", and "not established" are valid returns;
never pressure a member to fabricate a result or silently reinterpret acceptance as success.

**Weaker on this task, or not yet known.** Start with one bounded responsibility, complete relevant
context, an explicit output/check, and an early evidence check before a larger batch. Supply
steps or examples where needed; arbitrary context shortness can remove the information required
to succeed. Limit fan-out until the assignment is understood and reviewable. Reduce redundant
guidance when observed performance supports more method autonomy, without changing permissions.

After a failure, distinguish missing context, broken tools/access, bad decomposition, and a
demonstrated capability gap. Repair the relevant cause, narrow the assignment, or reassign to a
policy-eligible route with evidence for that responsibility. Do not climb a fixed model-strength
ladder or silently substitute routes. Retry an unchanged approach only for an evidenced transient
failure within the existing retry policy. If no capable solver or required verifier is available,
leave the conclusion unresolved and use the existing escalation route; weak votes cannot simulate
successful reasoning or satisfy review.

## Optimize delivery, including integration

Choose among qualified eligible roles using expected total delivery burden: briefing, model calls,
review, rework, and integration, while meeting quality and time constraints. Low call price alone
does not justify a helper. A single qualified execution owner can be cheaper than many helpers;
required independent review remains separate. Deterministic scheduling, formatting, and mechanical
merge/reduction can stay cheap. Ambiguous conflicts and semantic integration need a qualified
owner; a cheap scheduler must not decide them merely because it coordinates the run.

Reserve capacity for every mandatory review and required integration step before widening maker
work. Keep optional consultations and panels proportional to a named uncertainty and available
capacity; honor any consultation already required by the active mode. Diversity is useful when it
adds different evidence or approaches, not just different provider labels. Avoid all-to-all chat:
share the goal, interfaces, owner directory, and raw-evidence pointers, then route relevant findings
to their consumers. A polished summary is not a replacement for inspectable evidence.

Launch independent ready leaves concurrently within actual resource capacity. Reading the same
immutable source is not a write conflict or dependency. Represent information dependencies honestly:
if B needs A's result, B depends on A; shared mutable state needs ownership or isolation. Do not
invent dependency edges to hide scheduling preferences. A measured or credible estimated expert
review/integration bottleneck may justify a smaller batch even when model slots remain available.
For managed dispatch, set `max_concurrency` and `serialization_reason.kind: resource_limit` with
concrete `detail` naming the bottleneck, supporting observation/estimate, and revisit condition
(for example, one integrator can inspect two outputs per cycle; widen when its queue clears).
Coordinator convenience, vague coordination fear, and shared read-only sources do not qualify.
Capacity limits never waive dependency, write-scope, evidence, or review checks.

At fan-in, inspect raw evidence, assumptions, counterexamples, uncertainty, and deviations. Preserve
material disagreement and trace which outputs support the conclusion. Majority vote, correlated
agreement, and proxy improvement are not validation. Rising rework, review backlog, contradictory
evidence, or error propagation calls for changing the brief, partition, or in-flight width before
adding more members. Use existing task evidence to compare quality, latency, total cost, and rework;
no universal management ratio or numerical capability threshold is established here.

## Modes govern planning cadence

Default autonomously chooses decomposition and topology within the user's authorization and runs
its existing result/insight/self-evolution gates. Fast follows the human's direction and plan,
executes the bounded cycle, and returns to its existing checkpoint before the next dependent plan.
An executor may choose methods inside either mode's assignment. A failed human-plan premise returns
with evidence to the checkpoint; it does not authorize silently changing the goal or acceptance.
Planning cadence is independent of capability and team size: either mode may need one expert,
several experts, or support members. Fast does not automatically mean weak models or serial work;
Default does not require a large team. Preserve all mandatory reviews and mode-switch contracts.
Naive's isolated baseline does not load this reference.

## Evidence boundary

The package research dossier, `docs/research/team-management.md` and its evidence JSON, records
source versions, read scope, limits, and untested transfer claims; it is not required startup input.
Expert autonomy draws on [Aghion and Tirole](https://doi.org/10.1086/262063) and
[Dessein](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-937X.00227); review burden and exception routing on
[Garicano](https://doi.org/10.1086/317671); critical-task complementarity on
[Kremer](https://doi.org/10.2307/2118400). These are conditional organizational models, and the
dossier's Aghion–Tirole, Dessein, and Kremer checks cover original abstracts only.
Independent evidence sharing and expertise directories draw on
[Stasser and Titus](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.48.6.1467) and
[Lewis](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.88.4.587); honest exception reporting on
[Edmondson](https://doi.org/10.2307/2666999). Human-team mechanisms do not establish model behavior.
[Kim et al., v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08296v3) supplies bounded multi-agent counterevidence
to universal scaling claims; it is a preprint, not validation of these rules. Revise this policy
from project task evidence; no paid multi-model effectiveness comparison has been run for this change.
